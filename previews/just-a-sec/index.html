Mantel & bracket clocks

The one on the mantelpiece that went quiet.

  • Stopped after being moved, dusted or over-wound
  • Strikes the wrong hour, or the chime is out of step with the hands
  • Long overdue for a clean — old oil turns sticky over the years

Good to know: if it's key-wound, bring the key along.

Grandfather & longcase clocks

Tall, heavy, and part of the family.

  • Runs for a few minutes then stops — often out of beat after a move
  • Chimes silent, slow or at the wrong time
  • Weights, cables or pendulum need attention

Too big to bring in? Say so in your request so it can be talked through.

Wall & regulator clocks

Pendulum swinging, hands not moving.

  • Ticks unevenly, then stops — usually needs putting back in beat
  • Running noticeably fast or slow
  • Due for a full clean and service

Good to know: unhook the pendulum before you move it.

Book for my wall clock
III

Cuckoo clocks

The bird's gone shy.

  • Cuckoo stuck, silent or calling at the wrong time
  • Chains tangled or slipping off the wheel
  • Weights dropping too fast, or the clock stopping overnight

Good to know: tie up the chains before transporting it.

Book for my cuckoo clock
IV

Carriage clocks

Small brass case, fine old movement.

  • Stopped and won't restart after winding
  • Cracked or missing glass panel
  • Repeat or strike not working

Good to know: bring the travel case if you still have it.

Book for my carriage clock
V

Wristwatches

The watch in the drawer.

  • Quartz watch stopped — often just a battery
  • Mechanical or automatic losing or gaining time
  • Scratched glass, loose crown or a tired strap

Good to know: if you know whether it's quartz or mechanical, mention it.

Book for my watch
VI

Pocket watches

Handed down, wound up, stuck.

  • Won't run, or feels tight when winding
  • Cracked crystal or a loose hinge on the case
  • Needs a careful service after years in a drawer

Good to know: bring the chain or fob if it's attached.
